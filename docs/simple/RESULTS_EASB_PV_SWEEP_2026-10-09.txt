P_v SWEEP on EA-SB F2 Case 2 -- does the +8% bias track the lift?
==================================================================

NOT VALIDATION. Paper 2 publishes P_v = 2 D only. Every other row here is a
PREDICTION on both sides, and the comparison being made is internal: the
simplified model against the full model of the same layout, not either
against the paper.

THE QUESTION. The EA-SB family biases +8.4% high and does so with one sign
across all three published cases. Of the two candidate mechanisms left
standing after L113 was withdrawn, both are about how roller load reaches
the pipe -- continuously along a clamped shroud in the stand-in, through two
connectors in the real frame. If the offset DEPTH is what sets the size of
that error, the bias must move when the depth moves. This sweep moves it.

ONE PARAMETER MOVES. Case 2's layout is held exactly -- P_l1 = 10 D,
P_l2 = 2.5 D, P_c1 = 10 D, kB = 3.1, F2 connectors -- and only `P_v` runs
from 1 D to 4 D. P_v is measured from the pipe CENTRELINE, so the lift the
pipe actually gets is `P_v - OD/2`: 0.5 D to 3.5 D, a factor of seven.
4 D is `BaseStructure`'s shipped default and 2 D is the published case.

FOUR RUNS PER ROW, not two:
  - `tools/stiffness_rig.py` on the real ILS          -> EI_eq, E_eq
  - the contact profile on the real ILS               -> V, L1, L2
  - the real ILS-EASB through a full passage          -> X_c, X_i, X_e
  - the GD-Simple stand-in through a full passage     -> Xb, Xe

The real side goes through `study_easb_cases.run_case` UNCHANGED, not a copy
of it, so the reference column is the same code path Sec. 5's table came
from.

All three rig checks clean on every row: linearity under +0.05%, boundary
exact to the printed digits, plain-pipe control +0.0000%. The two tapers are
symmetric in every row (L2 catenary side = L2 vessel side to six decimals),
so one L2 is reported.

THE FRAME STIFFNESS IS NOT MONOTONIC IN P_v, which the rig found without
being asked. It peaks at P_v = 2.5 D (7.913 x EI_pipe) and falls off BOTH
ways -- 7.177 at 1 D, 7.725 at 4 D. The span is 4.0640 m throughout and kB
is 3.1 throughout, so this is geometry: the taper is fixed at P_l2 = 2.5 D
while the depth it has to climb grows, and past about 2.5 D the frame is
paying more in taper angle than it gains in depth. The range is only +10%
low-to-peak, which matters for reading the result below -- the stiffness is
very nearly a constant across this sweep while the lift changes sevenfold.

THE LIFTED FOOTPRINT GROWS AND THE TAPER GROWS FASTER. L1 is the component's
own deep length and does not move (9.996 D). L2 is measured off the contact
profile, not assumed, and it runs 1.249 -> 2.187 D, a 75% increase, because
a deeper offset takes longer to come back down to the arc. Total footprint
12.494 -> 14.370 D. `body_covers_shroud` is FALSE in all six: the stiffened
span is 10 D and the lifted footprint is 12.5 to 14.4 D.

THE RESULTS
-----------
ALL SIX SWEEP 100% OF THEIR TRAVEL, on both the real and the simplified
side.

`ratio` is the simplified peak against the real model's X_c, which is the
real peak in all six. The simplified peak is in Xe in all six -- the
simplified model has no connector, so it has no X_c to put a peak in; that
is the comparison Sec. 6 has used throughout and it is not changed here.

THE BIAS IS FLAT. +5.8% to +6.4%, a band of 0.6 POINTS, across a sevenfold
change in lift. Meanwhile the thing being predicted moves by a factor of
2.60: real X_c 1.305% -> 3.390%, simplified peak 1.3809% -> 3.5864%, and the
two multiply by 2.597 and 2.597 respectively. The reduction tracks a 160%
change in the answer and keeps its error constant to within half a point.

WHAT THIS RULES OUT. Any mechanism whose magnitude scales with the offset
depth, with the lift, with the taper severity, or with the lifted footprint.
All four change substantially over this sweep -- lift 7x, taper footprint
+75%, total footprint +15% -- and the bias does not move. In particular the
contact geometry over the lifted span cannot be the driver: a flat standing
in for a cylinder is a fixed geometric difference, but its CONSEQUENCE would
have to grow with how much of the pipe is sitting on it, and it does not.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT TOUCH. Both surviving candidates are about WHERE load
enters the pipe, not how deep the structure is, and neither predicts any
dependence on P_v:

  THE LOAD PATH. The real roller bears on the frame and reaches the pipe at
  two connectors; the stand-in's shroud is clamped and transfers
  continuously over 12.5 to 14.4 D. That difference exists in full at every
  P_v in this table. A flat bias is CONSISTENT with it and is not evidence
  for it.

  THE STIFFENED SPAN. The reduction stiffens between the connectors only.
  Whether a base bearing along its whole length also puts bending stiffness
  into the pipe outside that span is untested, and nothing here tests it.

Testing either one needs the span or the attachment changed, not the depth.
`P_c1` against `P_l1` is the sweep that separates them and it has not been
run.

A SECOND, SMALLER READING. X_i -- the pipe between the two connectors,
shielded by the frame spanning over it -- moves +10% (0.1131% -> 0.1244%)
while X_c moves +160%. The interior is almost indifferent to how deep the
structure is, which is what "shielded" should mean and is the first time it
has been measured across a depth range.

CONTROL
-------
P_v = 2 D reproduces the published Case 2 row on all four numbers already on
record: EI/EIp 7.870, E_eq 1653 G, real X_c 2.053%, simplified Xe 2.1855%.

The simplified figure prints 2.1854 here against 2.1855 on record, and the
difference is the INPUTS, not the code. The recorded row was run from the
measurement table's printed values (E = 1653 GPa, L1 = 9.996 D, L2 = 1.875 D);
this sweep feeds the measurement's own floats through. Run both ways on this
build: rounded inputs 2.185482%, measured floats 2.185435%, a difference of
4.7e-5 percentage points, with E low by 0.386 GPa and L1 and L2 rounded up
by 2.5e-4 and 4.7e-4 D. `sweep.repose` landed one commit before this sweep
and a refactor of a re-pose can move a number quietly, so it was ruled out
rather than assumed.
