P_c1 x P_l1 on EA-SB F2 Case 2 -- the sweep that separates the two candidates
=============================================================================

NOT VALIDATION. Paper 2 publishes (P_c1, P_l1) = (10 D, 10 D) for Case 2 and
(10 D, 15 D) for Case 3. Every other point is a PREDICTION on both sides, and
the comparison being made is internal: the simplified model against the full
model of the same layout.

WHY THIS GRID SEPARATES THEM. After L113 was withdrawn and L118 eliminated
the lift, two candidates were left for the EA-SB family's high bias. Both are
about where roller load enters the pipe, so both would show as a dependence
on how far the base reaches beyond the connectors. They differ at ONE place:

  THE STIFFENED SPAN candidate: the reduction is wrong because it stiffens
  BETWEEN THE CONNECTORS ONLY, while a real base bears along its whole
  length. At P_c1 = P_l1 there IS no base beyond the connectors, so this
  candidate predicts the bias GOES AWAY on the matched line.

  THE LOAD PATH candidate: the reduction is wrong because the stand-in's
  shroud is clamped to the pipe and transfers roller load CONTINUOUSLY, while
  the real frame reaches the pipe at two points. That difference is in full
  force at P_c1 = P_l1 as well, so this candidate predicts the bias SURVIVES
  on the matched line.

THREE LINES crossing at the published Case 2. P_l2 = 2.5 D, P_v = 2 D,
kB = 3.1 and the F2 connection system are held throughout.

  ratio line    P_l1 = 10 D fixed, P_c1 = 5, 7.5, 10        c/l 0.50 .. 1.00
  base line     P_c1 = 10 D fixed, P_l1 = 10, 12.5, 15, 20  c/l 1.00 .. 0.50
  matched line  P_c1 = P_l1 = 5, 10, 16, 20                 c/l 1.00

kB is a RATIO, not a stiffness, so the measured EI is not constant along any
line. The simplified model takes the MEASURED value at every point, so each
row remains its own matched pair.

15 D IS MISSING FROM THE MATCHED LINE and the reason is geometric, not a
choice -- see THE 15 D HOLE at the end. 16 D stands in for it.

FINDING 1: THE BIAS SURVIVES ON THE MATCHED LINE.
--------------------------------------------------
Four lengths at c/l = 1.00 -- 5, 10, 16 and 20 D -- give +3.7, +6.4, +6.4 and
+2.5%. Mean +4.8%, and NOT ONE POINT IS NEAR ZERO. When P_c1 = P_l1 there is
no base outboard of the connectors for the reduction to have mis-stiffened,
and the reduction is still high by about 5%.

THE STIFFENED SPAN IS ELIMINATED AS THE SOLE CAUSE. It is not eliminated as a
contributor -- it could still be feeding the scatter -- but it cannot be what
produces the offset, because the offset is there when the thing it blames is
absent by construction.

FINDING 2: THE BIAS DOES NOT TRACK c/l EITHER.
-----------------------------------------------
If the overhang of base beyond the connectors were the ordering variable, the
bias would rise as c/l falls. It does not, on either line:

  ratio line  c/l  1.00 -> 0.75 -> 0.50    +6.4  +5.1  +10.5
  base line   c/l  1.00 -> 0.80 -> 0.67 -> 0.50    +6.4  +4.6  +8.4  +0.4

Neither is monotone, and the two disagree about which end is worse. The
overhang is not an ordering variable for this error. Together with Finding 1
that is the second, independent strike against the stiffened-span
explanation.

WHAT IS LEFT, AND WHAT IT IS WORTH
-----------------------------------
Of the three candidates stated since this family was first reduced -- the
shroud (L113, refuted by L114), the lift (L118, eliminated), the stiffened
span (eliminated here as a sole cause) -- the LOAD PATH is the only one still
standing. It is also the only one that predicts what is observed: a bias
present at every point of the grid, including where P_c1 = P_l1, because the
stand-in transfers roller load continuously along 8.7 to 23.7 D of clamped
shroud at every one of those points while the real frame reaches the pipe at
two nodes.

THAT IS NOT PROOF AND IS NOT WRITTEN DOWN AS ONE. Being the last candidate
standing is a statement about the list, not about the mechanism. Testing it
needs the transfer itself changed -- a stand-in that ties the pipe at two
points instead of bearing along a shroud -- which is a different reduction,
not a different layout, and is not in this program.

The honest summary of the grid is: a residual of about +5% that does not
order by any of the geometric variables swept, with roughly +/-4 points of
scatter around it over nine points and no replication.

FINDING 3: THE REAL PEAK MIGRATES, ONCE.
-----------------------------------------
At (10 D, 20 D) the full model's peak LEAVES THE CONNECTOR: X_e 1.203%
against X_c 1.173%, a 2.5% margin. It is the first point in the EA-SB set
where X_c is not the peak, and it moves the quoted figure from +2.9% (against
X_c) to +0.4% (against the real peak).

The methodology point is the one that matters: "peak against peak" has to
mean the MAXIMUM OVER REGIONS on the real side, not X_c, and until this grid
the two were the same number everywhere so nothing distinguished them. Both
columns are printed above rather than one being chosen silently.

SECONDARY READINGS
------------------
THE MEASURED STIFFNESS IS NOT ORDERED BY kB. kB is held at 3.1 everywhere and
the rig reads EI/EI_pipe from 5.015 to 9.586 -- nearly a factor of two. It
rises with P_c1 at fixed P_l1 (5.015 -> 6.127 -> 7.870) and falls with P_l1 at
fixed P_c1 (7.870 -> 6.880 -> 6.289 -> 5.616), and on the matched line it is
non-monotone (5.855, 7.870, 9.586, 9.269 -- it turns over between 16 and
20 D). Same character as the P_v non-monotonicity in L118, from the same
cause: kB specifies a ratio per member and the frame's geometry decides what
that is worth in bending across the span.

X_i RISES WITH THE MATCHED LENGTH: 0.1276% at 5 D, 0.1167% at 10 D, 0.1361%
at 16 D, 0.1563% at 20 D. The shielded interior is nearly indifferent to
depth (L118) but not to length.

REAL X_c FALLS AS THE BASE LENGTHENS at fixed connector span -- 2.053% ->
1.801% -> 1.447% -> 1.173% over P_l1 10 to 20 D -- which tracks the measured
EI falling over the same line. That is a full-model trend, not a reduction
artifact, and it is why the base line's absolute strains are the lowest in the
grid.

THE 15 D HOLE
-------------
P_c1 = P_l1 = 15 D is REFUSED by the mesher -- `AssemblyError`, frame nodes of
degree > 2 -- and the reason is an exact geometric coincidence in the mirrored
`BaseStructure`:

  the outer connector slot sits at   P_c1/2 + P_c2  =  (2/3) P_c1   (P_c2 = P_c1/6)
  the top corner of the frame at     P_l1/2 + P_l2

These are the same point when (2/3) P_c1 = P_l1/2 + P_l2, which at
P_c1 = P_l1 and P_l2 = 2.5 D is P = 6 * P_l2 = 15 D EXACTLY. Two distinct
frame nodes then land on one position, the closed outline acquires a node
where three members meet, and `assemble._chains` cannot order the chain. The
refusal is CORRECT -- the topology really is ambiguous -- and it is a
coincidence of this grid, not a property of the matched line: (10 D, 15 D) is
Case 3's own layout and builds and runs fine, because its outer slot is at
6.667 D and its corner at 10 D.

The refused band has WIDTH, because nodes merge within `parts.MERGE_TOL` =
0.01 m rather than only when exactly coincident:

  P_c1 = P_l1      14.8 D    14.9 D    15.0 D    15.1 D    15.2 D
  slot - corner   -13.6 mm   -6.8 mm    0.0 mm   +6.8 mm  +13.6 mm
  verdict            ok      REFUSED   REFUSED   REFUSED     ok

|slot - corner| = |P_c1/6 - P_l2|, so the band is P in (14.852, 15.148) D,
width 0.295 D. Nothing was nudged into it or out of it: 16 D was used instead
and the hole is recorded.
