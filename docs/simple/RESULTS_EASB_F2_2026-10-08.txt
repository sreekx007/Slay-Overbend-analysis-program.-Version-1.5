ILS-SIMPLE for EA-SB F2 -- the first genuinely combined reduction
=================================================================

NOT VALIDATION. The "EA-SB" column is Sec. 5's own row for the full model on
this build; the paper column is Paper 2's published value.

WHY THIS ONE IS DIFFERENT. EA-ST needed a measured stiffness and no shroud.
EA-SB needs BOTH: its stiffness is in a frame tied at two connectors, AND it
lifts the pipe. Measured, not assumed: `contact_at` on an ILS-EASB returns
y = P_v with owner GD-SB over the base, against the plain 0.2032 m outside.

THE TWO MEASUREMENTS
--------------------
Stiffness by `tools/stiffness_rig.py` between the pipeline-side connector
nodes; lift by the same contact-profile sampling `report.regions` uses.

case          P_c1  P_l1    kB |   L_span  EI/EIp    E_eq     lin     bnd |    V/D    lift   L1/D  L2/D  tot/D
F2 Case 1     5.0D 10.0D  2.50 |   2.0320   4.183    878G +0.011% -0.000% |  2.000  609.6m  9.996 1.875 13.747
F2 Case 2    10.0D 10.0D  3.10 |   4.0640   7.870   1653G +0.026% -0.000% |  2.000  609.6m  9.996 1.875 13.747
F2 Case 3    10.0D 15.0D  3.10 |   4.0640   6.289   1321G +0.032% -0.000% |  2.000  609.6m 14.999 1.870 18.740

Case 3 is LESS stiff than Case 2 (6.289 against 7.870) at the same connector
span and the same kB, because its base is longer (P_l1 15 D against 10 D) and
that is a different frame. The rig picks this up without being told.

THE SHROUD IS REPRODUCED AS MEASURED, not forced to the body length. For a
GD-TP the two coincide by construction; here they are genuinely independent --
the stiffened span is the connector span (5 D, 10 D, 10 D) while the lifted
footprint is 13.7 D or 18.7 D. `body_covers_shroud` is FALSE in all three,
which is the first time that flag has fired on a real case and is exactly
what it was built to report.

THE SHROUD HYPOTHESIS IS REFUTED, and it was recorded only one commit ago.
L113 proposed that the GD-TP family's -2% bias came from the shroud --
"matched depth is not matched contact geometry", a flat standing in for a
cylinder -- on the evidence that the two EA-ST cases, which have no shroud,
straddled zero. EA-SB HAS a shroud and biases +8.4%, the OPPOSITE WAY from
GD-TP's -2.2%. A single mechanism in the shroud cannot produce a bias that is
negative in one family and positive in another. The hypothesis is dead; it is
marked so in L113 rather than quietly dropped.

WHAT IS LEFT, stated as candidates and not as findings:

  THE LOAD PATH DIFFERS, and only for EA-SB. In the real structure the roller
  bears on the FRAME, which reaches the pipe only at the two connectors. In
  the stand-in the shroud is clamped to the pipe and transfers roller load to
  it CONTINUOUSLY along its length. That is a materially different support
  condition over a 13.7 D footprint and it has no counterpart in the GD-TP
  family, where the body IS the pipe and transfer is continuous in both.

  THE STIFFENED SPAN MAY BE WRONG. The reduction stiffens only between the
  connectors, which is right for a top structure attached at two points. A
  base structure bears along its whole base, and whether that puts bending
  stiffness into the pipe outside the connector span has not been
  established either way here.

Both are testable and neither has been tested. Nothing in this file should be
read as explaining the EA-SB offset.

ADDED 9 Oct 2026. A THIRD candidate -- that the error scales with how deep
the structure holds the pipe -- has since been tested on Case 2 and
ELIMINATED: `P_v` swept 1 D to 4 D, a sevenfold change in lift, moves the
bias from +5.8% to +6.4% while the quantity predicted moves by a factor of
2.60. See `RESULTS_EASB_PV_SWEEP_2026-10-09.txt`. The two candidates above
are untouched by it -- neither predicts a `P_v` dependence -- and both are
still untested.
